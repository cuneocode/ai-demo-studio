Item 1. Business

Overview

Halcyon Interconnect, Inc. ("Halcyon," the "Company," "we," "us," or "our") designs, manufactures, and sells high-speed optical interconnect modules and liquid-cooled rack systems for accelerated-compute data centers. Our common stock trades under the symbol HCYN. This document is a synthetic, fictional annual report prepared for a demonstration; it is not an SEC filing, and Halcyon is not a real company.

We were incorporated in Delaware in 2011 and are headquartered in Fremont, California. We completed our initial public offering in June 2019. Our fiscal year is the calendar year, and fiscal 2025 ended on December 31, 2025.

We operate in two reportable segments. Optical Interconnect sells pluggable optical transceivers, linear-drive pluggable optics, and active electrical cables that connect GPU servers, switches, and storage inside AI clusters. Rack Systems sells liquid-cooled rack systems, coolant distribution units, cold-plate assemblies, and rear-door heat exchangers that allow operators to deploy dense GPU racks above 120 kilowatts per rack.

Fiscal 2025 revenue was $4,112.6 million, an increase of 38.0% from $2,980.1 million in fiscal 2024. Gross margin was 41.2%, compared with 39.1% in fiscal 2024. Net income was $802.5 million, or $4.77 per diluted share.

Our Strategy

Our objective is to be the leading independent supplier of the optical and thermal infrastructure that accelerated-compute clusters require. We pursue that objective through five strategic priorities.

First, lead each speed transition. We began volume shipments of our HX-1600 1.6-terabit transceivers in the third quarter of 2025 and intend for 1.6T products to exceed half of Optical Interconnect revenue by the end of 2026.

Second, extend from modules to engines. We are sampling our first co-packaged optics engine with two switch-silicon partners and expect qualification revenue in 2027. Co-packaged optics could reduce the market for pluggable modules over time, and we are investing so that we participate in that transition rather than being displaced by it.

Third, sell the rack, not only the part. Our HR-series liquid-cooled rack systems combine our coolant distribution units, manifolds, and cold plates with our optical cabling into a factory-integrated rack, which shortens customer deployment from weeks to days.

Fourth, diversify our customer base. We aim to reduce the share of revenue from our three largest customers below 50% by 2028 by expanding with neocloud operators, sovereign AI programs, and enterprise private-AI deployments.

Fifth, secure supply. We are adding a second indium phosphide laser supplier qualification, expanding our Penang, Malaysia facility, and opening rack integration capacity in Guadalajara, Mexico.

Products and Technology

Optical Interconnect. Our HX-800 and HX-1600 families are OSFP pluggable transceivers for 800-gigabit and 1.6-terabit Ethernet and InfiniBand links. They use electro-absorption modulated lasers built on indium phosphide laser die and digital signal processors purchased from third parties. Our LX linear-drive pluggable optics remove the digital signal processor to lower power per link. Our active electrical cables serve short in-rack links.

Rack Systems. Our HR-series racks support direct-to-chip liquid cooling for GPU servers. Each rack includes a coolant distribution unit, quick-disconnect manifolds, leak detection, and telemetry. Our CDU-800 row-level coolant distribution unit supports up to 800 kilowatts of heat removal. Rack Systems products carry standard warranties of three years, with extended warranties of up to five years available.

Research and development. We spent $452.4 million on research and development in fiscal 2025, or 11.0% of revenue. Our engineering centers are in Fremont, California; Austin, Texas; Penang, Malaysia; and Eindhoven, the Netherlands.

Artificial Intelligence in Our Business

Demand for our products is driven primarily by investment in artificial intelligence infrastructure. Large language model training and inference clusters require far more optical links per GPU, and far more heat removal per rack, than traditional cloud data centers.

We also use artificial intelligence in our own operations. Machine-learning vision models inspect optical sub-assemblies on our Penang production lines, which raised first-pass yield on HX-1600 modules from 81% to 92% during 2025. We use machine-learning demand forecasting to plan laser die purchases. In 2025 we deployed generative AI assistants for field service engineers, running on a private model endpoint in our own data center, governed by an AI use policy approved by our Audit Committee that prohibits entering customer confidential data into public AI services.

Customers

Our customers are hyperscale cloud providers, GPU cloud operators, original design manufacturers that build servers for those providers, and network equipment manufacturers. Our three largest end customers, each a hyperscale cloud provider, accounted for 27%, 19%, and 15% of fiscal 2025 revenue, or 61% in total, compared with 58% in fiscal 2024. We refer to them as Customer A, Customer B, and Customer C.

Our backlog was approximately $3.4 billion at December 31, 2025, compared with $2.1 billion a year earlier. Most backlog is expected to ship within twelve months, but customers may reschedule or cancel orders with limited penalties.

Manufacturing and Supply Chain

We manufacture optical modules in our owned facility in Penang, Malaysia, and through a contract manufacturer in Thailand. We integrate HR-series racks in Austin, Texas, and, beginning in the fourth quarter of 2025, in Guadalajara, Mexico.

Indium phosphide laser die are the most constrained input to our optical products. We currently purchase substantially all of our laser die from two suppliers, one of which is located in Japan and supplied approximately 70% of our fiscal 2025 volume. We also depend on a small number of suppliers for digital signal processors, quick-disconnect couplings, and cold plates.

In optical interconnect we compete with Coherent Corp., Lumentum Holdings Inc., InnoLight Technology, Eoptolink Technology, and Applied Optoelectronics, Inc., and with the in-house optics efforts of some customers. In liquid cooling and rack systems we compete with Vertiv Holdings Co., Schneider Electric, nVent Electric plc, and server original design manufacturers. We are also subject to conflict-minerals disclosure rules, the EU RoHS and REACH directives, and data privacy laws that apply to the telemetry our racks report.

Item 1A. Risk Factors

Investing in our common stock involves risk. The risks below could materially affect our business, financial condition, results of operations, and stock price. Because this is a synthetic report, these risks are illustrative.

Risks Related to Our Customers and Markets

We depend on a small number of hyperscale customers, and the loss of, or a reduction in orders from, any of them would materially reduce our revenue.

Our three largest customers accounted for 61% of fiscal 2025 revenue. These customers have substantial negotiating leverage, do not commit to long-term purchase volumes, and may qualify additional suppliers, design their own optics, or change architectures. A decision by Customer A alone to shift volume to a competitor could reduce our annual revenue by more than $1 billion.

Demand for our products depends on continued growth in artificial intelligence infrastructure spending, which may slow or reverse.

Our growth since 2023 has been driven by hyperscaler and GPU-cloud capital spending on AI clusters. If AI adoption slows, if model efficiency improvements reduce the need for compute, if customers digest previously purchased capacity, or if financing for data center construction tightens, orders for our products could fall sharply. Our revenue fell 14% in fiscal 2020 during a prior data center digestion period.

Architecture transitions, including co-packaged optics and linear-drive optics, could reduce demand for our pluggable products.

Switch and GPU vendors are developing co-packaged optics that place optical engines next to the silicon. If co-packaged optics are adopted faster than we expect, demand for pluggable transceivers, which represented most of Optical Interconnect revenue in fiscal 2025, could decline before our co-packaged engines are qualified.

Pricing pressure could reduce our gross margin.

Average selling prices for a given optical product typically decline each year. Our gross margin depends on introducing new products at higher prices and reducing costs faster than prices fall.

Risks Related to Our Supply Chain and Manufacturing

We rely on two suppliers for indium phosphide laser die, and a shortage would limit our ability to ship optical products.

Indium phosphide laser die are produced by few suppliers worldwide, and capacity is constrained. One supplier, located in Japan, provided approximately 70% of our fiscal 2025 laser die volume. A fire, earthquake, export restriction, quality failure, or allocation decision at that supplier could halt production of our highest-revenue products for months, because qualifying a new laser source typically takes nine to twelve months. Indium and other inputs are also subject to export controls imposed by China.

Our manufacturing is concentrated in Malaysia, and disruptions there would harm us.

Most of our optical module production occurs in Penang, Malaysia. Natural disasters, power or water shortages, political events, public health crises, or changes in trade policy affecting Malaysia could disrupt production.

We may not expand capacity quickly or efficiently enough.

We plan capital expenditures of approximately $550 million to $650 million in fiscal 2026 for Penang expansion, Guadalajara rack integration, and test equipment. Delays, cost overruns, or yield problems on new lines could cause us to miss customer demand or lower our gross margin.

Risks Related to Our Products

Defects in our liquid-cooled rack systems could cause coolant leaks and damage to customer equipment, resulting in warranty claims, liability, and reputational harm.

Our Rack Systems products circulate coolant through manifolds and quick-disconnect couplings next to GPU servers worth several million dollars per rack. A leak, coupling failure, or coolant contamination could damage customer equipment and cause outages. Our warranty terms generally cover repair or replacement of our products but customers may seek damages for their equipment and lost service. Our warranty reserve was $64.8 million at December 31, 2025, compared with $38.1 million a year earlier, reflecting higher Rack Systems volume and a field remediation program for a coupling seal in an early HR-series production lot. Because our rack products have a limited field history, actual warranty costs may exceed our estimates.

Our optical products are complex and may contain defects that are discovered only after deployment.

Defects can cause link failures in large AI training jobs, which can be costly for customers. We could be required to replace large volumes of modules, pay penalties, or lose future business.

Legal, Regulatory, and Compliance Risks

Export controls and trade restrictions have reduced, and may further reduce, our ability to sell to certain customers.

U.S. export controls on advanced computing items and related technology, and any future restrictions on optical components, could prevent sales to customers in China and other countries, require licenses that may be denied, or prompt foreign governments to retaliate against U.S. suppliers. Violations of export control laws can result in significant penalties.

Tariffs and changes in trade policy could increase our costs.

We import components into the United States, Malaysia, Thailand, and Mexico. New or increased tariffs, including those affecting goods assembled in Mexico, could raise our costs or make our products less competitive.

Environmental regulation of coolant fluids could require product changes.

Restrictions on per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances and certain refrigerants could limit the fluids our Rack Systems products use and require costly redesign or requalification.

Cybersecurity incidents could disrupt our operations or expose customer data.

Our racks report operating telemetry, and our systems hold customer roadmaps and designs. A cyberattack on us or our suppliers could interrupt manufacturing, compromise data, or allow tampering with product firmware.

Risks Related to Our Common Stock

The market price of our common stock has been and may continue to be volatile.

The price of our common stock has fluctuated significantly and may decline because of changes in AI infrastructure sentiment, customer announcements, quarterly results, or market conditions. In fiscal 2025 our stock traded between a low of $41.20 and a high of $96.85.

Sales of our common stock by our officers and directors, or the perception that such sales may occur, could cause our stock price to decline.

Many of our officers and long-tenured employees hold significant amounts of our common stock with a low cost basis. Our Insider Trading Policy, filed as Exhibit 19.1, permits officers and directors to trade only during open trading windows following pre-clearance, or under trading plans that comply with Rule 10b5-1 under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, including the required cooling-off period before the first trade. The policy prohibits short sales, transactions in publicly traded options, and pledging of our stock, and requires General Counsel pre-clearance of any hedging transaction such as a collar. Our executive officers are affiliates, so their sales are also subject to Rule 144 volume and manner-of-sale limits. Large or concentrated sales, including sales under Rule 10b5-1 plans, could depress our stock price.

We do not intend to pay dividends for the foreseeable future.

We intend to retain earnings to fund growth. Investors may need to sell shares to realize a return.

Item 1C. Cybersecurity

Risk Management and Strategy

We maintain a cybersecurity risk management program aligned with the NIST Cybersecurity Framework. The program covers our corporate network, our manufacturing systems in Penang, Thailand, Austin, and Guadalajara, product firmware, and the telemetry service our racks use. We perform annual third-party penetration tests, assess critical suppliers, and operate a security operations center that monitors identity, endpoint, network, and cloud telemetry around the clock. We segment manufacturing networks from corporate networks and sign all product firmware.

We have adopted controls for our internal use of generative AI, including inspection of prompts and responses for sensitive data on our private model endpoint, logging of AI assistant activity, and restrictions on the data that may be sent to hosted AI services.

We did not identify any cybersecurity incident in fiscal 2025 that materially affected, or is reasonably likely to materially affect, our business strategy, results of operations, or financial condition.

Governance of Cybersecurity Risk

Our Chief Information Security Officer, who has more than twenty years of security experience, leads the program and reports to our Chief Financial Officer. The Audit Committee of our Board oversees cybersecurity risk and receives a report from the Chief Information Security Officer each quarter.
